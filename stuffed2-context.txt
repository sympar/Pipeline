PSYCHOLOGICAL ARCHITECTURE
The Bimbo Arc:
Aggie's transformation follows the classic bimbofication fetish narrative: intelligent/female agent undergoes physical and mental simplification to become a sexually optimized object. The "wiping away" refers to the erasure of her former self — personality, cognition, reservations — replaced by pure sexual availability.

The Consent Paradox:
The narrative introduces sophisticated tension around consent. Aggie chooses transformation, but the process itself compromises her capacity to withdraw consent once begun. Each "checkpoint" asking if she's okay arrives when she's increasingly unable to say no — chemically aroused, cognitively diminishing, and past the point of return. This creates a fetishization of inescapable consent — willing but irrevocable.

The Musical Frame:
The original song transforms the bimbofication from mere visual fetish into narrative spectacle. Music serves as diegetic element — Aggie doesn't just transform; she performs her own dissolution. The "dancing dolls" suggest that this transformation is celebratory and performative, not tragic.

The Object's Satisfaction:
The narrative resolves with Aggie's subjective fulfillment despite objective loss. She "got the guy she wanted" — but as an object he uses, not an equal partner. This reflects agalmatophilic fantasy: the ultimate romantic success achieved through self-elimination as subject.

The "Stuffed" Series Continuity:
Stuffed 2 replaces the original's Claire and Edgar with Aggie and Chester, suggesting an anthology structure around transformation themes. Where the original focused on voodoo doll/cumflation mechanics, the sequel introduces musical structure and bimbofication as its specific transformation mode.
